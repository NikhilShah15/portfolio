Bike Rental Demand & Usage Analysis

An end-to-end data analytics project analyzing 10,886 hourly bike rental records to understand demand patterns, seasonal trends, weather effects, temperature relationships and customer behavior.

Python SQL Tableau

1. Executive Summary

This project analyzes historical bike rental data to understand the key factors influencing rental demand and identify opportunities for better operational planning.

The analysis found that rental demand varies significantly by time of day, season, temperature, weather conditions and customer type.

Two major peak-demand windows were identified: 7–9 AM and 4–7 PM. Registered users form the majority of the customer base, while casual users show greater sensitivity to unfavorable weather conditions.

2. Project Objectives

  • Identify the hours with the highest and lowest rental demand.
  • Analyze rental demand across different seasons.
  • Understand the relationship between temperature and rental demand.
  • Analyze how weather conditions influence rental activity.
  • Compare casual and registered customer behavior.
  • Translate analytical findings into actionable business recommendations.

3. Data & Methodology

Data Used

The project uses a historical Bike Sharing / Bike Rental dataset containing 10,886 hourly observations.

Data Area Variables
Time datetime, date, hour, month, day
Demand count
Customer Type casual, registered
Season season
Weather weather
Environment temp, humidity, windspeed
Calendar workingday, holiday

Data Preparation

Python was used to clean and transform the raw dataset into an analysis-ready format. Additional variables were created including hour, season labels, weather labels, day type, customer ratios and temperature buckets.

Analytical Approach

Python was used for data preparation and exploratory analysis. SQL was used to answer business questions through aggregation and conditional analysis. Tableau was then used to create the final dashboard and communicate the findings visually.

Workflow: Raw Data → Python → SQL → Tableau → Insights → Recommendations

4. Key Findings & Visualizations

Finding 1 — Rental Demand Peaks During Specific Hours

The x-axis represents the hour of the day (0–23), while the y-axis represents the average number of bike rentals.

Two clear peak-demand windows can be identified:

  • Morning Peak → 7–9 AM
  • Evening Peak → 4–7 PM

Rental activity is lowest during the late-night and early-morning period.

Business Importance

Capacity Planning: The business should ensure maximum bike availability during the peak windows of 7–9 AM and 4–7 PM.

Maintenance Planning: Suitable low-demand windows within 9 PM–5 AM can be used for routine maintenance and servicing without interfering with the main demand peaks.

Figure 1. Average Bike Rental Demand by Hour

Finding 2 — Rental Demand Varies by Season

Fall shows the highest average rental demand, followed by Summer, Winter and Spring. The customer mix also changes across seasons.

Business Importance

Capacity Planning: Ensure sufficient bikes and staff during high-demand seasons, especially Fall and Summer.

Seasonal Promotions: Offer discounts, special deals or seasonal packages during lower-demand periods such as Spring to encourage more rentals and improve fleet utilization.

Since the registered-customer share remains relatively consistent across seasons, promotional efforts can focus more heavily on casual customers.

Figure 2. Average Bike Rentals and User Composition by Season

Finding 3 — Temperature Influences Rental Demand

The analysis shows a positive relationship between temperature and bike rental demand, with the 30–40°C range showing the highest observed average demand.

Business Importance

Demand Planning: Temperature can be included alongside time, season and weather when estimating expected demand.

Operating Decisions: During favorable temperature conditions, the business could adjust operating hours or increase service availability to capture additional demand.

Targeted Marketing: Temperature-based campaigns can promote bike rides when conditions are favorable.

Figure 3. Relationship Between Temperature and Rental Demand

Finding 4 — Weather Strongly Affects Casual User Participation

The analysis shows that the registered-user share remains relatively high across all weather conditions, while the casual-user share decreases as weather becomes more unfavorable.

Casual users decrease from approximately 18% in clear weather to around 4% in severe weather. Registered users therefore form a more stable customer base across weather conditions.

Business Importance

Casual Customer Targeting: Focus marketing efforts on casual users during favorable weather conditions.

Weather-Based Promotions: Introduce targeted offers, weekend packages or seasonal promotions when weather conditions are favorable.

Registered Customer Retention: Continue engagement and retention programs for registered customers, who remain the core customer segment.

Figure 4. User Type Ratio Across Weather Conditions

Finding 5 — Registered Users Dominate Overall Rentals

Registered users account for approximately 82.9% of total rentals, while casual users account for approximately 17.1%.

This shows that registered customers form the core customer base and contribute the majority of rental activity.

Business Importance

Customer Retention: Focus on retaining registered users through membership benefits and loyalty programs.

Membership Conversion: Target casual users with membership trials or introductory plans to encourage conversion into registered customers.

Customer Segmentation: Use the different behaviors of casual and registered users to develop more precise marketing strategies.

Figure 5. SQL Analysis of Casual vs Registered User Contribution

6. Actionable Recommendations

1. Capacity & Maintenance Planning

Maximize bike availability during the 7–9 AM and 4–7 PM peak periods, while using suitable low-demand windows within 9 PM–5 AM for maintenance and servicing.

2. Seasonal Capacity & Promotions

Maintain sufficient bikes and staff during Fall and Summer while using seasonal offers and packages during lower-demand periods such as Spring.

3. Temperature-Based Planning

Include temperature in demand planning and consider adjusting operating hours, service availability and marketing activity during favorable temperature conditions.

4. Weather-Based Customer Strategy

Focus promotions on casual customers during favorable weather conditions while maintaining retention and engagement programs for registered customers.

5. Customer Retention & Membership Conversion

Strengthen loyalty programs for registered users and use membership trials or introductory plans to encourage casual users to become registered customers.

7. Conclusion

The analysis shows that bike rental demand is strongly influenced by time of day, season, temperature, weather and customer type.

The hourly analysis identified clear morning and evening peak periods, creating opportunities for better capacity planning, while low-demand hours can be used for maintenance activities. Seasonal analysis showed higher demand during Fall and Summer, while lower-demand periods such as Spring provide opportunities for seasonal promotions.

Temperature and weather patterns can be incorporated into demand planning and operating decisions. The weather analysis also showed that casual customers are more sensitive to unfavorable weather, while registered customers remain the core and more stable customer segment.

Overall, the project converts historical bike rental data into practical business insights and actionable recommendations covering capacity planning, maintenance planning, seasonal and weather-based marketing, operating decisions, customer retention and membership conversion.
